# Image Briefs — Tier 10 / October 2026 posts

**Ten images: 5 heroes + 5 infographics.**

> **Read `IMAGE-STYLE-GUIDE.md` first. It overrides anything here.** Section 2 decides who appears, section 3 what the warehouse looks like, section 9 how to write the prompt.

## Status

All five posts are written, built and **held**: `noindex`, out of `sitemap.xml`, no card in `blog.html`. They stay held until these images exist, because a post with a broken hero is worse than no post. Upload the ten files and they can be published.

The sixth piece in this batch, the Q4 peak season refresh, **needs no new image.** Its new content is a table of spot freight rates dated 1 October 2026. Rates move weekly, and a number baked into an image is stale within days and expensive to change. It stays as an HTML table that can be updated in seconds.

## Conventions

| | |
|---|---|
| **Heroes** | generate **16:9 at 2K**, crop to **2070×760**. Subject in the middle band |
| **Infographics** | generate **2:3 or 3:4 portrait at 2K**, resize to **1200×1500**. Highest quality setting |
| **Format** | `.webp`, sRGB, heroes under ~150 KB |
| **Upload to** | `images/blog/`, exact lowercase filenames below |
| **Alt text** | already live in the built pages. The image must match it |

## Variety, on purpose

You asked last batch whether the homepage images all looked the same. This set is deliberately varied in shot type, so the five heroes do not read as one image five times:

| Post | Shot | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| EU customs reform | Medium, at a desk | **The customer's home office in Europe** |
| Section 301 | **Close-up on hands and a carton marking** | Packing bench |
| Honest 3PL comparison | Medium, walking | **A visitor on the warehouse floor** |
| Importer of record | Medium, two figures | **The dispatch doorway, daylight behind** |
| 3PL vs forwarder | **High angle, looking down** | Consolidation area |

## Who appears

Section 2 of the style guide draws the line that matters here.

**Images of our operation**: Chinese staff aged 22 to 27, mixed gender, **orange polos** with a small chest logo, green epoxy floor, blue racking. Heroes 2, 4 and 5.

**Images of the customer**: the person should look like the reader, a Western small-business owner. Hero 1 is set entirely at the customer's desk in Europe, because the EU reform is about what the seller now owes, not about our warehouse. Hero 3 puts a **visiting** customer on our floor alongside our staff: the visitor is not a warehouse worker and must not wear the orange uniform.

**Never, anywhere:** hard hats, hi-vis vests, Western warehouse workers, US-style green and orange racking, courier livery, government seals, flags.

> **Check first whether a real photograph covers any of these.** A genuine photo of your team beats a generated one every time. The dispatch doorway in hero 4 is a scene you could photograph for real in an afternoon.

## 1. `eu-customs-reform-importer-distance-sales-2026.webp`

**Alt (live):** "A European online seller at their desk reviewing the product descriptions, values and tariff codes on an order before it ships to EU customers"

```
SCENE: A bright home office in a European city apartment. A tall window on the left with soft daylight, a pale wooden desk, a bookshelf behind, softly out of focus.

SUBJECT: A woman in her thirties who runs a small online brand sits at the desk studying a laptop screen closely. One hand rests on the trackpad, the other holds a printed product specification sheet she is checking against the screen. Concentrating, not smiling at the camera.

DETAILS: On the desk, an open kraft box holding a single product sample, a notebook with a pen, a cup of coffee.

LIGHT: Soft natural daylight from the window, gentle and even.

STYLE: Documentary photography, 50mm, eye level, taken from about two metres away. Shallow depth of field, the background soft. Natural and unposed.

COMPOSITION: The woman and the laptop in the middle horizontal band, centred vertically. Wide horizontal composition.

EXCLUDE: no readable text on the screen or the paper, no EU flag or ring of stars, no government seals or crests, no customs uniforms, no brand logos, no warehouse, no stock-photo smiling at camera.
```

**Why this scene:** the reform makes the seller the importer. The image is the seller doing the new homework: checking that what the order says matches what is in the box. It is also the only hero in this batch outside our building, which breaks up the set.

## 2. `section-301-forced-labor-tariff-china-2026.webp`

**Alt (live):** "A warehouse worker in Shenzhen checking the country of origin marking on a carton against the commercial invoice before it ships to the United States"

```
SCENE: A packing bench in a Shenzhen fulfilment warehouse. Green epoxy resin floor and blue steel racking visible behind, softly out of focus. Stacked yellow-brown kraft cartons.

SUBJECT: A close view of a young Chinese woman in her mid twenties, wearing an orange company polo shirt with a small logo on the left breast. She holds a printed invoice in one hand and touches a stencilled marking on the side of a kraft carton with the other, comparing the two.

DETAILS: The stencilled marking on the carton reads exactly "MADE IN CHINA" in plain black capitals. A small barcode label sits beside it, unreadable.

LIGHT: Bright even overhead light with a warm task lamp over the bench.

STYLE: Documentary photography, 50mm, slightly above eye level, about one metre away. Shallow depth of field: the marking, her hand and the invoice sharp, everything else soft. Natural and unposed.

COMPOSITION: The carton marking, her hand and the invoice in the middle horizontal band, centred. Wide horizontal composition.

EXCLUDE: no readable text other than "MADE IN CHINA", no US flag, no government seals, no customs logos, no hard hats, no hi-vis vests, no Western workers, no third-party brand logos, no stock-photo smiling at camera.
```

**Why this scene:** the article's sharpest practical point is that origin is where goods are made, not where they ship from. A hand on an origin marking says that without a word of copy.

**Text check:** the only legible string is `MADE IN CHINA`. Read it letter by letter before uploading.

## 5. `china-3pl-vs-freight-forwarder-vs-sourcing-agent.webp`

**Alt (live):** "Two warehouse workers in Shenzhen combining cartons from several different suppliers onto a single pallet, checking each one against a printed packing list"

```
SCENE: The consolidation area of a Shenzhen fulfilment warehouse, seen from a raised position as if from a mezzanine. Green epoxy resin floor with yellow demarcation lines, blue steel racking along the edges, fluorescent strip lights.

SUBJECT: Two young Chinese warehouse staff in their mid twenties, a woman and a man, both in orange company polos with a small chest logo, building one pallet from cartons that visibly come from different suppliers: different sizes, different shades of kraft, different coloured tape. He places a carton on the pallet; she checks it against a printed packing list on a clipboard.

DETAILS: Three smaller part-pallets of mixed cartons wait beside them. A roll of stretch film on a stand.

LIGHT: Bright even overhead fluorescent light.

STYLE: Documentary photography, 35mm, high angle looking down at about 45 degrees from roughly four metres away. Natural and unposed.

COMPOSITION: The pallet being built and both staff in the middle horizontal band, centred. Wide horizontal composition.

EXCLUDE: no readable text or supplier names, no logos on cartons, no hard hats, no hi-vis vests, no Western workers, no forklifts, no third-party brand logos, no stock-photo smiling at camera.
```

**Why this scene:** consolidation is the role most readers have never seen. Mismatched cartons becoming one pallet makes it obvious at a glance, and the high angle keeps it distinct from the homepage receiving dock photograph, which is shot at eye level.
