How Much Does China Fulfilment Cost? The 2026 Pricing Guide

By Noel Murphy Published August 29, 2026

"How much does it cost?" is the question every seller actually wants answered, and the one most fulfilment providers bury behind a contact form. So here is a straight answer, with real numbers. China fulfilment is priced in a handful of lines: pick and pack, storage, any prep or value-added work, and shipping. The handling side is genuinely cheaper at the source than in the West; the shipping side is the big variable and depends on your product and destination. This guide breaks down each line, shows what we charge, and flags the hidden costs that make a cheap headline rate expensive in practice.

Quick answer: China fulfilment handling is low-cost: our pick and pack is $0.99 per order with no minimums, storage is $0.49 per CBM per day (first 30 days free), and there are zero receiving fees. Prep is priced per unit (labelling from $0.15, poly bagging from $0.30, kitting from $0.50), and standard visual inspection is free. Shipping is the biggest variable and is quoted by weight and destination. For comparison, Western 3PLs often charge around $2.75 just for the first item's pick and pack.

The handling lines are fixed and transparent. The one that moves is shipping, and it is worth being honest about why.

Why shipping is quoted, not fixed

Nobody can give you a single, honest "shipping from China costs $X" number, and you should be wary of anyone who tries. Shipping depends on the weight and dimensions of the item and the destination country, and the mode you choose:

  • Express courier (DHL, FedEx, UPS) for fast direct-to-consumer parcels. We pass on carrier discounts of up to 40% on standard rates.
  • Air freight for faster bulk movements, from roughly $4.50 to $8.50 per kg depending on lane and service. Our air vs sea vs express comparison sets out when each mode makes sense.
  • Sea freight for bulk inventory, with less-than-container-load from around $300 per CBM and full containers from approximately $1,800 for a 20-foot container to the US.

A light, small item ships cheaply by express; a heavy or bulky one may only make economic sense by sea. This is why a real quote beats a headline number: the same $0.99 pick and pack sits in front of wildly different shipping costs depending on what you sell and where it goes. Our express and freight options are quoted per shipment for exactly this reason.

How China compares with a Western 3PL

On handling and storage, China is generally cheaper, and the reason is simple: labour and warehouse space cost less at the source. Western 3PLs commonly charge around $2.75 for the first item in pick and pack alone, before storage and prep, against our $0.99 per order. Prep work, labelling, bagging, kitting, follows the same pattern, which is a large part of why sellers prep for Amazon FBA in China rather than paying destination rates.

Shipping is where it gets nuanced. Individual parcels from China can cost more per unit than domestic last-mile delivery inside the destination country. That is why the cost-optimal answer for a growing brand is usually not "all China" or "all destination", but a mix: import your proven sellers in bulk and fulfil them locally, and ship the long tail direct from China. We work through that maths in detail in our guide to shipping direct to FBA versus staging first.

The hidden costs that make a cheap rate expensive

A low pick-and-pack headline can hide the lines that actually cost you money. When you compare providers, ask about these:

  • Receiving fees. Some charge to book your stock in. Ours are zero.
  • Storage after any free period, and long-term penalties. A free first month means little if the ongoing rate is high or penalties bite. Ours is $0.49 per CBM per day with no long-term penalties.
  • Minimum-order charges. Minimums quietly raise your effective per-order cost at low volume. We have none.
  • Duty. This is the big one for 2026. With US de minimis ended and the EU's low-value rules changed, parcels that used to clear duty-free now owe duty. It has to be priced in, and ideally prepaid through DDP so the customer is never billed at the door and you are never surprised by it.

Add those up and the "cheapest" quote is often not the cheapest once the real cost lands.

A worked way to think about it

For a light e-commerce product shipped direct to a customer, your per-order cost is roughly: $0.99 pick and pack + any prep + the express shipping quote + prepaid duty. Storage is a small monthly line spread across your inventory, near zero in the first 30 days. Where you warehouse matters too: our Shenzhen vs Yiwu comparison shows how port proximity changes the shipping line. And if you are still weighing whether to outsource at all, we compare using a China 3PL against self-fulfilling. For bulk movements into a destination warehouse or Amazon, the model flips to a low per-unit sea-freight cost plus one bulk customs entry, then domestic fulfilment at the other end. Which is cheaper per order depends on your volume, weight and where your customers are, and that is precisely the calculation a quote answers and a blog post cannot.

The bottom line

China fulfilment is transparently cheap on the parts you control, $0.99 pick and pack, $0.49 per CBM per day storage with 30 days free, free inspection, zero receiving fees, and priced honestly on the part you cannot fix, shipping, which depends on your product and destination. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does fulfilment from China cost per order?

The fulfilment handling itself is low: our pick and pack is $0.99 per order with no minimums, and storage is $0.49 per CBM per day with the first 30 days free and zero receiving fees. The larger and more variable cost is shipping, which depends on the item's weight and dimensions and the destination, so it is quoted per shipment rather than fixed. As a rough comparison, Western 3PLs commonly charge around $2.75 for the first item in pick and pack alone, so the handling side is markedly cheaper at the source.

What does China fulfilment pricing include?

A typical China fulfilment quote covers pick and pack, storage, and any prep or value-added work such as labelling, poly bagging, kitting and inspection, plus the shipping to the customer or destination. Our published rates are $0.99 per order pick and pack, $0.49 per CBM per day storage after a free first 30 days, FNSKU labelling from $0.15 per unit, poly bagging from $0.30 per unit, kitting from $0.50 per unit, free standard visual inspection, and zero receiving fees. Shipping is quoted separately by weight and destination.

Is fulfilment from China cheaper than a US or UK 3PL?

On handling and storage, generally yes, because labour and warehouse space cost less at the source, so pick, pack, prep and storage are lower than typical Western rates. The picture on shipping depends on volume and model: individual parcels from China can cost more per unit than domestic last-mile in the destination country, which is why higher-volume brands often import in bulk and fulfil locally for their proven sellers while running direct-from-China for the long tail. The right mix is a maths question, not a slogan.

What hidden costs should I watch for in China fulfilment?

The ones that catch sellers out are receiving fees, long-term storage penalties, minimum-order charges, and duty. Since US de minimis ended and the EU changed its low-value rules, duty now applies to parcels that used to clear free, so it must be priced in and ideally prepaid via DDP. Ask any provider whether receiving is free, whether there are minimums, what storage costs after any free period, and how duty is handled, so the headline pick-and-pack rate is not hiding the real cost.

How much is warehouse storage in China?

Our storage is $0.49 per CBM per day, charged by the cubic metre, with the first 30 days free for new inventory and no long-term penalties. Storing bulk stock at a low origin rate and drip-feeding it to a destination fulfilment centre such as Amazon is often far cheaper than holding everything at the destination, where storage, especially in Q4 peak periods, is considerably more expensive.
