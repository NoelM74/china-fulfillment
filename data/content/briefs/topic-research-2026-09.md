## 1. CBP Form 5106 and voided importer numbers

**Target keyword:** `cbp form 5106`
**Secondary:** importer of record number voided, IOR accuracy CBP, form 5106 requirements 2026
**Intent:** urgent informational, shading into panic
**Working title:** *CBP Is Voiding Importer Numbers from 18 September. Is Your Form 5106 Right?*

### Why this one first

It takes effect **three days from now**, on 18 September 2026, and almost nobody selling from China knows about it.

CBP published a Federal Register notice on 19 August 2026. From 18 September it will **immediately void an importer of record number** where the Form 5106 data is inaccurate or incomplete. A voided number cannot be used to enter merchandise. CBP has said it is reviewing the data behind every IOR number on file, whether opened last month or decades ago. It is part of the initial implementation of Executive Order 14411, Strengthening Customs Enforcement.

### The part that hits your audience directly

The physical address on Form 5106 **cannot** be any of these:

- a registered agent address
- a customs broker address
- a freight forwarder address
- a PO box
- a business service centre
- any other party's address

Phone and email must belong to the importer too, not the broker or forwarder.

That is exactly what a large share of overseas sellers have on file, because a broker or forwarder set the account up for them years ago. Those numbers are now voidable, and the first they will know is a shipment that cannot clear.

### Strategic fit

This is the strongest internal linking opportunity on the site. **"Importer of record" already appears on 16 pages with nowhere to send the reader.** Every one of those becomes an inbound link on day one.

It also reinforces the editorial line the site already takes: the client is the importer of record, not us. This is that line with a deadline attached.

### Honesty constraints

We cannot fix anyone's Form 5106 and must not imply it. The post advises checking the data and going to a licensed customs broker or trade attorney to correct it. What we legitimately own: our address is not a valid physical address for their 5106, and we should say so explicitly, because some clients may have used it.

### Verified sources
Federal Register notice 2026-16911, 19 August 2026. Clark Hill client alert. CBP bulletin vol. 60 no. 26, 2 September 2026.

## 2. EPR and the EU packaging rules

**Target keyword:** `epr compliance ecommerce`
**Secondary:** PPWR 2026, EPR packaging EU sellers, LUCID registration, authorised representative EPR
**Intent:** commercial investigation, high anxiety
**Working title:** *EPR and the EU Packaging Rules: What Changed on 12 August 2026*

### Why

**Zero coverage on the site.** Checked with a word-boundary search: EPR appears nowhere. Nor does PPWR, extended producer responsibility, authorised representative, or GPSR.

The Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (EU 2025/40) has applied since **12 August 2026**, and from that date **online marketplaces carry expanded legal obligations to verify seller EPR compliance**. That is the bit that turns a dry regulation into a live problem: listing suspension, withheld payouts, fines, market exclusion.

There is no single EU registry. You register separately in every country you sell into: LUCID in Germany, Citeo in France, CONAI in Italy, Ecoembes in Spain. Sellers without an EU legal entity must appoint an Authorised Representative.

### Strategic fit

Slots directly beside the existing Germany/EU, UK and EU de minimis posts, all of which currently stop short of EPR. Also connects to packaging decisions, which is genuinely our territory via kitting and packaging work.

### Honesty constraints

We are **not** an Authorised Representative and cannot register anyone with LUCID or Citeo. The post must say so plainly and point to specialist EPR services. What we can honestly offer is accurate packaging data: material types and weights per SKU, which is what an EPR filing is built on and what most sellers cannot produce.

### Optional scope decision
GPSR and the EU Responsible Person requirement is a closely related gap (also zero coverage). Either fold it in as a second section, or hold it as a fifth post. My view: fold it in, since the reader's question is "what do I need for the EU", not "what is EPR".

## 3. China to UAE and Saudi Arabia

**Target keyword:** `china to uae fulfillment`
**Secondary:** saudi arabia ecommerce fulfillment from china, GCC fulfillment, shipping to middle east from china
**Intent:** commercial, market entry
**Working title:** *China to UAE and Saudi Fulfilment (2026): VAT, Duties and the COD Reality*

### Why

There is a market-by-market post for **Japan, Australia, Canada, UK, Germany and the US**. There is none for the Middle East, despite `cod-service.html` naming Saudi Arabia and the UAE as served markets with local couriers and weekly settlement.

So this is a genuine capability with no content behind it, in a pattern the site already runs well.

### Facts worth building on

- Saudi duties run roughly 5 to 25 percent by HS code
- Saudi VAT is 15 percent on CIF value, and there is **no low-value VAT exemption**; everything is taxable
- Saudi de minimis for personal imports sits at SAR 1,000
- UAE VAT is 5 percent
- Selling cross-border from the UAE into Saudi triggers Saudi tax obligations rather than counting as one market, which trips up sellers who assume the GCC is a single bloc

### Strategic fit

Links cleanly to the COD service page and the Southeast Asia COD post, and completes the market-coverage set. COD is the genuine differentiator here and we already run it.

### Honesty constraints

VAT registration and tax obligations are the seller's, as with every other market post. Confirm current country list with ops before publishing, since the COD page says coverage is expanding.

## 4. Subscription box fulfilment from China

**Target keyword:** `subscription box fulfillment`
**Secondary:** subscription box fulfilment from china, recurring order fulfillment, subscription box 3PL
**Intent:** commercial, actively shopping for a provider
**Working title:** *Subscription Box Fulfilment from China: Assembly, SKU Rotation and the Forecasting Problem*

### Why

The homepage already sells subscription boxes. Six pages mention them. **No page targets the term.** That is a straightforward mismatch between what we claim and what we rank for.

The market is real: roughly $38 billion in 2025, growing 14 to 18 percent annually, with 54 percent of US households holding at least one subscription.

### The angle

The interesting problem is not packing boxes, it is that you order stock weeks ahead against a subscriber count that keeps moving between order date and ship date. Plus SKU rotation between cycles, compressed shipping windows where everything goes out at once, and variable item counts per box.

Source-side assembly answers most of that, and the existing kitting page supports the claim.

### Strategic fit

Purely commercial intent, closest to revenue of the four. Links to kitting, crowdfunding, multi-channel and the pricing page.

### Honesty constraints

Light. This is a capability we genuinely have. Avoid inventing retention or churn benefits we cannot substantiate.

# Considered and rejected

**Mercado Libre and Mexico.** Tempting on paper: Mercado Libre opened its first mainland China fulfilment centre in December 2025, holds about 97 percent penetration in Mexico, and runs a Global Selling programme across Mexico, Brazil, Chile, Colombia and Argentina. **Rejected because the capability is not evidenced.** The COD page names Southeast Asia and the Middle East; nothing on the site supports LATAM operations. Writing it would mean claiming a market we cannot show we serve. Worth revisiting if ops confirms LATAM lanes.

**Q4 air freight and peak season costs.** The 2026 numbers are dramatic: airline fuel spend forecast up nearly 40 percent, peak season surcharges reaching $2,000 per FEU on transpacific lanes in June, booking delays of three to seven days. **Rejected as a new post because `q4-peak-season-fulfillment-from-china-2026` already targets it.** Better as an update to that post than a competing page.

**Amazon holiday peak fees.** Peak fulfilment fees run 15 October 2026 to 14 January 2027, averaging $0.32 per unit above standard, with the 3.5 percent fuel surcharge applying on top. **Rejected because it overlaps both the Q4 post and the inbound placement fees post.** Also better as an update.

**GPSR on its own.** Genuine gap, but the reader's question is "what do I need to sell in the EU", not "what is GPSR". Folding it into the EPR post serves that better than splitting it.
