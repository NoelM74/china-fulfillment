# The Best China 3PL Depends on What You Ship. An Honest Comparison, Including Where We Lose.

![A brand owner walking the Shenzhen warehouse floor with a member of staff, looking at the racking and packing stations before choosing a fulfilment partner](/images/blog/best-china-3pl-companies-2026.webp)

> **Quick answer:** There is no single best China 3PL, because the providers are different kinds of business. **Cross-border carrier networks** such as YunExpress and Cainiao are built to move huge volumes of light marketplace parcels cheaply. **Warehouse 3PLs** such as SendFromChina, ChinaDivision, NextSmartShip and us are built to hold stock and handle it: QC, kitting, branded packaging, crowdfunding. If you ship high volumes of small parcels, a carrier will likely beat us on price. If you need stock in the US or EU as well, NextSmartShip has the larger overseas network. Choose by what you ship, not by anyone's ranking, including this one.

Search for the best 3PL in China and you will find several "top 10" lists. Read who wrote them. Most are published by a 3PL, and most put their author at number one.

This one is written by a 3PL too. So we have not ranked anyone. Instead, here is what each kind of provider is actually built for, what each says about itself, and the cases where the others are a better choice than we are.

## First, two different kinds of business

The single most useful thing to know is that "China 3PL" covers two quite different models, and comparing across them is how sellers end up disappointed.

| | Cross-border carrier network | Warehouse 3PL |
|---|---|---|
| **Core business** | Moving parcels out of China at scale | Holding and handling your stock |
| **Built for** | High volumes of light parcels | Goods that need work done on them |
| **Strengths** | Line haul rates, network reach, speed at scale | QC, kitting, branded packaging, supplier coordination |
| **Warehousing** | An add-on to the shipping | The centre of the service |
| **Typical customer** | Marketplace sellers shipping thousands of parcels a day | Brands, crowdfunding creators, FBA sellers |

Plenty of sellers use both. A warehouse 3PL holds and prepares the goods and hands parcels to a carrier network to move them. The question is not which model is better. It is which one your business needs at the centre.

## Where the others beat us

This is the section most comparison articles leave out. Here is ours.

**On parcel rates at very high volume, a carrier network will usually win.** YunExpress states it handles more than a million shipments a day and runs its own freighters. We do not operate aircraft and we are not going to match a network that size on the cost of moving a light parcel. If your product is small, uniform, needs no handling, and you ship thousands a day, start with a carrier.

**If you need stock in the US or Europe as well, NextSmartShip has the bigger network.** It states more than 20 warehouses across 13 countries. We fulfil from China and pair with a separate local warehouse when fast domestic delivery matters, which is the [hybrid model](china-us-hybrid-fulfillment-model-2026.html) we have written about. If a single provider across several countries is what you want, they are set up for it.

**If you want a listed company, SFC is one.** SendFromChina's parent is listed on ChiNext, with the disclosure that comes with it. We are a private company. For some buyers, particularly larger ones with procurement rules, that matters.

**If an ISO 9001 certificate is a requirement, ChinaDivision states it holds ISO 9001:2015.** We do not hold ISO 9001. Our credentials are a national AAAA logistics enterprise rating and membership of the Amazon Service Provider Network, which are different things, and if your process requires ISO 9001 specifically, we are not the fit.

**For board games specifically, SFC runs a dedicated service.** Board games have their own handling quirks, from component counts to box crush. A provider that has built a service around them deserves a look.

**We publish our prices.** As at 3 October 2026, the three other warehouse 3PLs above ask for a quote rather than publishing per-order pricing on their homepages. Ours is on the site: **$0.99 pick and pack per order with no minimums**, $0.49 per CBM per day for storage, and **no receiving fees**. You can price us before you ever speak to us. **Supplier problems get a visit, not an email.** Our team speaks Mandarin to your suppliers and English to you, in the same city as the factories. When a supplier ships short or ships wrong, it is documented within hours and sorted the same business day. That is the core of our [supplier management](supplier-management-china.html) and the reason many of our clients chose us over a carrier.

**QC happens before anything moves.** Goods are checked on arrival, with photographs, before they are stored or shipped. If quality control at source matters to your product, see how our [QC and FBA prep](quality-control-fba-prep-china.html) works.

**We have been doing it since 2010.** Fifteen years in Shenzhen does not make us the right choice. It does mean we have seen most of the ways this goes wrong.

## How to choose, in five questions

1. **How much work does each order need?** None beyond a label: lean towards a carrier. Kitting, inserts, branded boxes, QC: a warehouse 3PL.
2. **How many parcels a day, and how heavy?** Thousands of light parcels favour a carrier's scale. Fewer, heavier or more varied orders favour a 3PL.
3. **Where are your customers, and how fast do they expect delivery?** If they expect two-day domestic delivery, you need stock near them, whether through a provider's overseas network or a hybrid.
4. **How many suppliers feed your stock?** Several factories shipping to one place is where on-the-ground consolidation and supplier coordination earn their keep.
5. **Can you see the price before you commit?** If a provider will not tell you what an order costs until you are deep in a sales process, ask why.

Then do what we tell every prospective client: **run a pilot with a small batch before you move everything.** Our guide on [how to choose a China 3PL](how-to-choose-a-china-3pl.html) covers the questions to ask and the red flags to watch for.
