# 3PL, Freight Forwarder, Sourcing Agent or Consolidator? Who Does What When You Buy from China

![Two warehouse workers in Shenzhen combining cartons from several different suppliers onto a single pallet, checking each one against a printed packing list](/images/blog/china-3pl-vs-freight-forwarder-vs-sourcing-agent.webp)

> **Quick answer:** They do different jobs at different points. A **sourcing agent** finds and manages the factory before goods exist. A **consolidator** combines goods from several suppliers into one shipment. A **freight forwarder** moves bulk cartons, pallets and containers. A **3PL** holds your stock and ships each individual order to your customer. A **customs broker** clears the goods at the destination. Many companies do more than one, so ask which job is their core. If you sell direct to consumers from China you need a 3PL; if you ship bulk to Amazon you need a forwarder; if you do not have a supplier yet, you need a sourcing agent first.

A common way to waste a week: ask a freight forwarder, a 3PL and a sourcing agent to quote for "shipping my products from China", then try to compare the three prices.

You cannot, because each of them quoted for a different job. One priced moving a container. One priced shipping individual orders. One priced finding you a factory. None of them was wrong. The question was.

## Five roles, in the order your goods meet them

Every product bought from China passes through some or all of these. Knowing which stage you are at tells you who you need.

| Stage | Role | What it does | You need it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Make** | Sourcing agent | Finds factories, negotiates, follows production | You have no supplier, or want a better one |
| **Combine** | Consolidator | Merges goods from several suppliers into one shipment | You buy from more than one factory |
| **Move in bulk** | Freight forwarder | Books ships and aircraft for cartons, pallets, containers | You send stock in bulk to a warehouse or Amazon |
| **Ship each order** | 3PL | Stores stock, picks, packs and ships individual orders | You sell direct to customers |
| **Clear** | Customs broker | Files the import entry and calculates duty | Goods enter the destination country |

![Who does what when you buy from China: a sourcing agent finds the factory, a consolidator combines suppliers, a freight forwarder moves bulk freight, a 3PL ships each order, and a customs broker clears the border](/images/blog/infographic-who-does-what-china.webp)

## The sourcing agent

**Works before your goods exist.** A sourcing agent finds and vets factories, gets quotes, negotiates price and terms, and follows production through to completion. Many arrange inspections before the goods leave the factory.

**What it usually does not do:** store your stock or ship orders to your customers. Its job is largely finished when the goods are made and paid for.

**You need one** when you do not have a supplier yet, when you want to move away from a supplier that is letting you down, or when you are buying in a category you do not know well. Check how an agent is paid before you start, and whether any of that money comes from the factory side rather than yours.

## The consolidator

**Combines several small shipments into one.** If you buy from five factories, a consolidator receives all five deliveries at one warehouse, checks them, and sends them on together. Freight forwarders also consolidate in a different sense: combining several customers' cargo into one shared container.

**Consolidation is a service, not usually a company type.** You will rarely hire "a consolidator" on its own. It is something a forwarder or a 3PL does, and the difference in cost between five separate freight bills and one combined shipment is often where it pays for itself. That is exactly the problem our [D2C consolidation service](china-consolidation.html) exists to solve, and for Amazon sellers our [FBA consolidation](amazon-fba-consolidation.html) does the same into Amazon's network.

## The freight forwarder

**Moves goods in bulk.** A forwarder books space on ships and aircraft, handles export documentation, and arranges for cartons, pallets or containers to get from China to a destination warehouse. Some issue their own bills of lading; most do not own the ships or aircraft themselves.

**What it usually does not do:** ship individual orders to your customers. A forwarder delivers your stock to a warehouse, whether that is Amazon's, a US 3PL's or your own. What happens to each unit after that is someone else's job.

**You need one** when you are moving stock in bulk: a container to a US warehouse, cartons into Amazon FBA, pallets to a European distribution centre. Our [freight forwarding service](international-freight-forwarding-china.html) covers that leg for clients who also use our warehouse.

## The 3PL

**Holds your stock and ships each order.** A third-party logistics provider receives your goods, stores them, and when a customer orders, picks the item, packs it and ships it to that customer. Most connect directly to your store so orders flow in automatically. Many add kitting, inserts, branded packaging and returns.

**A China 3PL** does this from China, shipping individual orders direct to customers worldwide by express and postal carriers. That is a different model from shipping bulk to an overseas warehouse first, with different costs and delivery times, and our [D2C vs US 3PL comparison](direct-from-china-d2c-vs-us-3pl-2026.html) works through when each makes sense.

**What a 3PL does not do:** become your importer of record by shipping your goods. That stays with you, which our piece on [who the importer of record is](importer-of-record-china-fulfillment-2026.html) explains.

## The customs broker, the one people forget

**Clears your goods at the destination.** A customs broker prepares and files the import entry, classifies the goods and works out the duty. In the United States, customs brokers must hold a licence from CBP.

It is the role sellers most often assume someone else has covered. A forwarder may arrange a broker for you, and some employ them, but the broker is the party actually filing the entry. When customs asks questions, it is the broker and the importer of record who answer them, not the warehouse that packed the box.

## The mix-ups that cost money

**"My forwarder will ship to my customers."** Usually not. A forwarder delivers bulk to a warehouse. If you want individual orders delivered, you need a 3PL at one end or the other.

**"My 3PL is my importer."** No. A fulfilment provider has no financial interest in your goods and does not become the importer of record by handling them.

**"My sourcing agent handles quality."** Some do, some do not, and "we visit the factory" is not the same as an inspection report with photographs. Ask what you will actually receive.

**"Consolidation is a separate supplier I need to find."** It is almost always a service inside a forwarder or a 3PL you may already be talking to.

## Which do you actually need?

| Your situation | Who you need |
|---|---|
| No supplier yet | A sourcing agent first |
| One supplier, selling direct to consumers | A 3PL |
| Several suppliers, selling direct to consumers | A 3PL that consolidates |
| Shipping bulk stock to Amazon FBA | A freight forwarder, ideally with FBA prep |
| Shipping bulk to your own overseas warehouse | A freight forwarder and a customs broker |
| Selling on several channels at once | A 3PL, plus a forwarder for any bulk leg |

## When one company does several

Plenty do, and there is nothing wrong with that. Having the consolidation, the forwarding and the fulfilment under one roof removes handovers, and handovers are where cartons go missing and blame goes round in circles.

The question worth asking is **which of these is the company's core business**. A forwarder that has added a small warehouse is a different proposition from a 3PL that also forwards. Ask what proportion of their work each service makes up. The answer tells you where they are strongest.
