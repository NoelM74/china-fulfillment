# China to Canada Fulfilment (2026): Duties, CARM and the CAD$20 Reality

![Parcels shipped DDP from a Shenzhen warehouse to Canadian customers clearing customs with duty and GST prepaid under CARM in 2026](/images/blog/china-to-canada-fulfillment-2026.webp)

Canada is a strong, underserved market for China-based brands, and also the one most likely to trip up a seller who assumes it works like the US or Australia. It does not. Canada's duty-free threshold for goods from China is tiny, its import system was overhauled in recent years, and getting either wrong means surprise charges at your customer's door or shipments stuck at the border. None of it is hard once you know the rules.

This is the 2026 guide to fulfilling Canadian orders from China: the CAD$20 reality, what CARM means for you, what your customers actually pay, and how to ship cleanly from Shenzhen.

> **Quick answer:** Canada's de minimis for goods from China is just CAD$20, so most orders are dutiable and taxable (GST 5% plus any provincial tax plus duty by HS code). Commercial importers of record must be registered on the CARM portal. Ship DDP so every charge is prepaid and your customer never sees a bill. Express from Shenzhen lands in about 5-9 days.

## The CAD$20 reality

Here is the number that surprises sellers: Canada's de minimis threshold for courier shipments from countries other than the US and Mexico, **which includes China, is CAD$20**. Below that, shipments are generally free of duty and tax. Above it, and CAD$20 is a very low bar, the order becomes dutiable and taxable.

The higher thresholds you may have read about, where shipments up to CAD$40 are tax-free and up to CAD$150 duty-free, apply only to goods shipped from the US and Mexico under the CUSMA trade agreement. Goods from China do not get them. For practical purposes, assume every Canadian order you ship from China above CAD$20 carries tax and possibly duty, and price accordingly.

Above the threshold, expect GST at 5% federally, provincial sales tax in some provinces, and customs duty at a rate that depends on the product's HS classification.

## What CARM means for you

Canada modernised how it handles commercial imports through CARM, the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management system. Since October 2024, importers of record must be registered on the CARM Client Portal. In practice that means obtaining a Business Number and an import-export (RM) program account, and settling duties and taxes through the portal on a structured monthly cycle.

If you import commercial goods into Canada as the importer of record, for example bulk stock into a Canadian warehouse, CARM registration applies to you, and it is your responsibility to arrange, though a licensed customs broker can act on your behalf. For direct-to-consumer parcels shipped DDP, the import clearance is handled as part of the shipment. As with all tax and customs matters, confirm your specific obligations with a Canadian customs broker or adviser.

## Why DDP is close to mandatory for Canada

Because Canada's threshold is so low, most of your orders will owe something, which makes [DDP](/ddp-shipping-from-china-explained-2026.html) the only sensible model for direct-to-consumer. Ship DAP and leave the customer to settle duty and tax at the door, and the courier adds its own handling fee on top of the charge, an experience that produces refused deliveries and complaints on a large share of your orders, not a rare few. Ship DDP and the duty, GST and any provincial tax are calculated and prepaid, so your customer gets one all-in price and a parcel that simply arrives. ## Delivery from Shenzhen

From a [Shenzhen warehouse](/ecommerce-fulfillment.html):

- **Express courier**, about 5-9 days to the customer's door, DDP.
- **Air freight**, roughly 6-12 days plus clearance, for faster bulk movements.
- **Sea freight**, around 20-35 days, for moving bulk stock to a Canadian warehouse or 3PL.

West-coast destinations near the Pacific ports tend to clear and deliver faster than the east. Selling into the other big English-speaking market too? For most brands, entering Canada by shipping direct from Shenzhen DDP is the low-risk start: no CARM importer setup of your own, no second warehouse, capital stays light. Canada rewards sellers who respect its quirks: a very low CAD$20 threshold, so nearly everything is taxable; CARM registration for commercial importers of record; and DDP as close to mandatory as it gets, because so many orders owe duty and tax.
