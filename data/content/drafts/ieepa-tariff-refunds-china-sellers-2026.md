# IEEPA Tariff Refunds for D2C and Crowdfunding Sellers: Who Gets the Money When You Shipped from China

![A seller checking printed receipts against figures on a laptop at a home desk, the kind of record-keeping a tariff refund claim depends on](/images/blog/ieepa-tariff-refunds-china-sellers-2026.webp)

> **Quick answer:** On **20 February 2026** the US Supreme Court ruled that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs, voiding the "fentanyl" and "reciprocal" tariffs collected on Chinese goods from 2025. CBP is refunding them through **CAPE**, a process in its ACE portal, and it pays by ACH to the **importer of record** on each entry. So one question decides whether money comes back to you: **whose name is on the entry as importer of record?** For most D2C parcels and crowdfunding rewards, that was the courier or the DDP shipping provider, not the brand. The refund goes to them first, and what reaches you depends on their policy and your contract. Section 122 and Section 301 duties are not part of CAPE.

Most of the money is moving. By mid-September 2026, CBP had accepted about **$134.7 billion** of potential and certified refunds into CAPE and certified about **$122 billion**, including interest, for payment by the Treasury.

If you sell direct to consumers from China, or shipped Kickstarter or Indiegogo rewards to US backers in 2025, you paid a share of that, usually inside a DDP shipping rate or a courier's duty bill. But the refund may not be yours to claim, because you were never the importer. That is the DDP trade-off coming due. This guide explains where the money goes for each way D2C sellers and creators shipped from China, what CAPE does not cover, and the deadlines that can quietly close a claim.

## What happened, in five dates

| Date | What changed |
|---|---|
| 20 Feb 2026 | Supreme Court rules 6–3 in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs |
| 24 Feb 2026 | A 10% global surcharge under Section 122 replaces the IEEPA tariffs, for 150 days |
| 20 Apr 2026 | CBP opens CAPE Phase 1 for IEEPA refunds |
| 24 Jul 2026 | Section 122 expires; a Section 301 forced labor duty of 12.5% on China-origin goods takes effect |
| 6 Oct 2026 | CAPE Phase 3 opens, for finally liquidated entries covered by court orders |

What replaced IEEPA for China is covered in our [Section 301 forced labor guide](section-301-forced-labor-tariff-china-2026.html). This article is about getting back what was paid before.

## How CAPE works

CAPE stands for Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries. The importer of record, or its customs broker, files a **CAPE declaration** in the ACE portal listing the entries it wants refunded. No supporting documents are needed when it is filed. CBP checks the entries, certifies the refund with interest, and the Treasury pays it **by ACH**.

| Phase | Opened | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | 20 April 2026 | Unliquidated entries, and entries no more than 80 days past liquidation |
| Phase 2 | 29 June 2026 | Entries flagged for reconciliation with no reconciliation entry yet filed, within the same 80-day limit |
| Phase 3 | 6 October 2026 | Finally liquidated entries, for importers who are plaintiffs in a refund case at the Court of International Trade and gave CBP a valid importer number by 30 July 2026 |

**Check your banking details first.** As at mid-September, **20,184 refunds worth $1.3 billion** were on hold because the importer of record had not given CBP its ACH banking information. Setting up ACH refunds in ACE is the step most often missed.

## The question that decides it: who was the importer of record?

Every formal US customs entry has an entry summary, CBP Form 7501, which names the **importer of record** and gives its importer number. That party answered to CBP for the duty, and it is the party CBP refunds.

It is often not the seller who paid for the goods, and that is the point most people miss. If you shipped DDP, the duty was bundled into a price you paid someone else, and CBP has no record of you at all. Ask your broker, forwarder or carrier for copies of the entry summaries for your 2025 and early-2026 shipments, and read the importer of record on each.

Our [importer of record guide](importer-of-record-china-fulfillment-2026.html) explains how the role works under each arrangement.

## Where the refund goes, by how you shipped

| How you shipped from China | Usually the importer of record | Where the refund goes | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| D2C parcels by express courier (UPS, FedEx, DHL), DDP or DAP | Depends on the entry: the carrier's brokerage or the customer as consignee | Usually to the carrier, then to whoever paid the duty | Check the entry, follow the carrier's process, do not file twice |
| D2C parcels on a DDP line run by a forwarder or shipping provider | The provider's US entity | To the provider | Ask in writing whether it has filed and whether it passes refunds on; check your contract |
| Crowdfunding rewards shipped to US backers | The courier or DDP provider, as above | To them; on DAP shipments, to whoever paid at the door, which may be the backer | Ask your provider; decide how you will handle any refund with backers |
| Postal parcels after de minimis ended | Depends on the carrier and how duty was collected | Ask the carrier or platform | Keep every invoice and tracking record |
| Bulk stock imported to a US warehouse under your own importer number | You | To you, by ACH | Check ACE, set up ACH, have your broker file the CAPE declaration |

**If a courier or DDP provider was the importer**, which covers most D2C sellers, you are not dealing with CBP, you are dealing with a supplier. The refund is legally theirs, and whether they owe it to you depends on what you agreed. A flat all-in DDP price that never itemised duty makes a pass-through harder to argue for. A quote that listed duty as a cost passed through at actual makes it easier. Get the answer in writing.

**If you ran a crowdfunding campaign**, there is a second question: who actually bore the cost. If you shipped DDP and paid the duty yourself, any pass-through is yours to chase. If backers paid duty at the door, a carrier refunding "whoever paid" may refund them, not you. And if you charged US backers a tariff surcharge in 2025, for example through Kickstarter's Tariff Manager, decide now how you will treat any refund that arrives. No CBP rule covers that; it is between you and your backers, so a short, early update is better than silence.

**If you were the importer**, for example because you also bring bulk stock into a US warehouse, this is straightforward. Your broker can pull your entries from ACE, file in batches and track each one. The main risk is time: an entry that drifts past the 80-day window without a protest may fall outside the phases you can use.

**If a courier was involved**, the carriers each published their approach in spring 2026, summarised by the Liberty Justice Center on 4 May:

- **UPS** said it would request refunds on customers' behalf and pass them to the payers, and that brokerage and processing fees charged when the tariffs were collected are not refundable.
- **FedEx** committed to file for all customers it served as customs broker, prioritising by liquidation date, and to pass refunds on to whoever bore the charges.
- **DHL** said it would refund only what it receives back from CBP. Where the customer was the importer, DHL said it would file only with the customer's authorisation, for a standard import paperwork fee.

Policies change, so check each carrier's current page. CBP said the first refund payments would go out around 11 to 14 May 2026, so anyone who promised money before then was not offering a real refund.

## What CAPE does not cover

- **The Section 122 surcharge.** The 10% surcharge from 24 February to 24 July 2026 was imposed under a different law. The Court of International Trade ruled it unlawful on 7 May 2026, but that relief covered only the named plaintiffs, and the appeal is pending.
- **Section 301 duties.** The China Section 301 lists and the 12.5% forced labor duty since 24 July 2026 remain payable. A challenge to the forced labor duty was argued at the Court of International Trade on 30 September 2026.
- **Normal MFN duty, and fees.** Ordinary duty was never in question, and brokerage or processing fees charged at the time are not refunded.

## The deadlines and traps

**Liquidation.** CBP normally liquidates an entry about 314 days after entry. Phases 1 and 2 cover entries up to 80 days past liquidation.

**Protests.** After liquidation, a protest must normally be filed within 180 days under 19 U.S.C. 1514. An entry that becomes final without a protest or a court case may be very hard to recover. The government has argued, in an appeal filed on 2 June 2026, that it can refund finally liquidated entries only for importers with a pending court case, which is why Phase 3 is limited to plaintiffs.

**Offers that are too good.** Watch for firms promising faster refunds in exchange for large contingency fees or an assignment of your refund rights. The Liberty Justice Center, which is litigating on behalf of importers, has warned against exactly this. A licensed broker can file a CAPE declaration for you at normal rates.

## A checklist for this month

1. **List your shipments** from China that cleared US customs from February 2025 to February 2026.
2. **Get the entry summaries** and find the importer of record on each.
3. **If it was you,** set up ACH refunds in ACE and ask your broker to file every eligible entry in CAPE.
4. **Check liquidation dates,** and ask your broker about protests for anything near or past the 80-day window.
5. **If it was a forwarder or carrier,** ask in writing whether they filed, when, and whether they will pass the refund on.
6. **Do not file twice** for entries a carrier is already claiming.
7. **Keep the records:** commercial invoices, packing lists, payment receipts and tracking numbers.

The IEEPA refund is real money, and most of it has already been certified. Whether any of it comes back to you depends on one line on a form you may never have seen. Find out who your importer of record was, set up ACH if it was you, ask in writing if it was not, and watch the liquidation dates, because they are what close a claim.
