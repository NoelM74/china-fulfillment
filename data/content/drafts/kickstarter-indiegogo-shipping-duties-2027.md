# Kickstarter and Indiegogo Shipping in 2027: How to Price Shipping, Duties and Tariffs into Your Campaign

![A creator at a desk with a laptop and an order list, closing a labelled shipping box, the moment each reward's shipping and duty cost has to be right](/images/blog/kickstarter-indiegogo-shipping-duties-2027.webp)

> **Quick answer:** Price shipping by region and weight, and decide **before launch** who pays import charges. For **US backers there is no duty-free threshold** any more: every reward pays duty, including the **12.5% Section 301 forced labor duty** on China-made goods since July 2026. Ship **DDP** and build the duty into pledges, or use Kickstarter's **Tariff Manager** for a per-item US surcharge. EU backers' parcels pay a flat **€3 per item category** since 1 July 2026, plus VAT. UK parcels of £135 or less need VAT charged at sale. Remember that platform fees apply to shipping you collect. Fulfilling direct from China avoids paying to ship everything to a third country first.

A campaign can hit its goal and still lose money if shipping and import charges were priced on old rules, or not priced at all.

If your last campaign shipped in 2024, the rules for US and EU backers have both changed since: the US ended duty-free imports for low-value parcels, and the EU added a new charge on them. This guide covers what changed by market, the one decision to make before launch, how to build a shipping price per backer, and how Kickstarter's and Indiegogo's tools fit in.

## What changed since your last campaign

| Backer market | The rule in 2027 | What it means for rewards from China |
|---|---|---|
| **United States** | No de minimis: suspended since August 2025 and repealed in law from 1 July 2027 | Every reward pays duty: the MFN rate, any Section 301 list duty and the 12.5% forced labor duty |
| **European Union** | Flat €3 per item category on low-value parcels since 1 July 2026, plus VAT | Ship DDP, with VAT handled through IOSS or the platform |
| **United Kingdom** | Orders of £135 or less: VAT charged at sale | Register for UK VAT or let the platform collect it; no doorstep bill |
| **Canada** | De minimis for goods from China is CAD$20 | Most rewards pay duty and GST; ship DDP |
| **Australia** | Orders of A$1,000 or less: GST collected at sale, no duty | Collect GST at checkout; no doorstep bill |

The US change is the one that catches creators out. A $35 reward that used to arrive duty-free now pays duty on arrival, and if you ship DAP, your backer is the one asked to pay it. Our guide to [shipping D2C orders from China to the US in 2027](shipping-d2c-orders-china-to-us-2027.html) explains why de minimis is not coming back.

## The decision to make before launch: DDP or DAP

**DAP** (delivered at place) means the backer pays the import charges at the door, usually with a carrier handling fee on top. It looks cheaper on your spreadsheet. In practice it produces refused parcels, one-star comments and refund requests, because nobody who backed a project expects a bill when the reward finally arrives.

**DDP** (delivered duty paid) means you settle the charges before delivery. You have to price for it, but the backer simply receives the parcel. For the US, Canada and the EU, ship DDP. For the UK and Australia, collect the VAT or GST at the point of sale. Our [DDP explainer](ddp-shipping-from-china-explained-2026.html) covers how it works from China.

Whichever you choose, **say it on the campaign page**. "Duties and taxes included" is a selling point. "Backers are responsible for import charges" is a warning, and some backers will read it as one.

## How to build a shipping price per backer

Work it out per reward tier and per region. These are the lines:

| Line | What drives it |
|---|---|
| Courier or postal rate | Packed weight and size, and the destination zone |
| Duty and import tax | The market's rules, your HTS code and the declared value |
| Pick and pack | Per order |
| Kitting | Per unit, if a reward combines several items or add-ons |
| Packaging | Box size, inserts and protection |
| Platform fees | A percentage of everything you collect, including shipping |
| Peak and fuel surcharges | When the rewards ship |

Here is the duty line for one example: a reward declared at **$40**, going to a US backer, for a product with a 0% MFN rate that sits on Section 301 List 4A at 7.5%.

| Duty layer | Rate | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| MFN duty | 0% | $0.00 |
| Section 301, List 4A | 7.5% | $3.00 |
| Section 301 forced labor duty | 12.5% | $5.00 |
| **Duty on this reward** | | **$8.00** |

That $8 has to come from somewhere: the pledge price, the shipping charge or a tariff surcharge. Your own rates depend on the HTS code. **Declare the true value of the goods.** Under-declaring to cut duty is the quickest way to have parcels held and penalties applied.

Two practical points on the other lines. Dimensional weight decides many courier rates, so a box only slightly too big can cost more than the product inside; our [DIM weight guide](dim-weight-packaging-optimisation-2026.html) explains it. And our published fulfilment prices are **$0.99 per order** for pick and pack, with standard mailers and small boxes included, and kitting from **$0.50 per unit**.

## Using Kickstarter's tools

**Pledge Manager.** Kickstarter's own pledge manager is now available to all creators at no additional upfront cost. You set shipping after the campaign, **by weight or flat rate**, by region, item type or reward level, and backers see the fee at checkout. Backers can update their address until you lock orders, and since a release on 1 June 2026 you can schedule an address lock for one group of backers, so each shipping wave locks just before it goes.

**Taxes.** Kickstarter says its Pledge Manager calculates, collects and remits item-level US sales tax and EU and UK VAT, and since April 2026 you can choose whether Kickstarter collects and remits VAT for you. If it does, ask how that VAT is shown on the shipping paperwork, so EU parcels are not charged VAT a second time at import.

**Tariff Manager.** Launched in April 2025 inside the Pledge Manager, it lets creators add **per-item surcharges for backers with US shipping addresses**. You request access through a banner in the Pledge Manager, and the surcharge shows as a separate line on the backer's payment page. It is a useful fix for a campaign that was priced before the tariff changes. For a new campaign, it is cleaner to price the expected duty in from the start, so backers see one honest number.

**Fees on shipping.** Shipping collected through pledges counts toward your total funds, so creator guides note that Kickstarter's **5% platform fee and payment processing fees** apply to it. Price for that too.

## Indiegogo after Gamefound

Indiegogo was acquired by Gamefound on 22 July 2025 and moved onto Gamefound's platform on 16 October 2025. Coverage of the move reported a flat 5% fee and stronger built-in pledge management. The pricing logic in this guide is the same on either platform: work out shipping and import charges per region, decide DDP before launch, and collect enough to cover both plus the fees.

If your campaign ends between November and January, plan around **Chinese New Year on 6 February 2027**. Factories wind down from mid-January and restart slowly through February. ## Your pre-launch checklist

1. **Weigh and measure a packed sample** of every reward tier.
2. **Get shipping quotes by region** for those weights.
3. **Work out duty per market**, including the 12.5% forced labor duty for the US.
4. **Decide DDP**, and say "duties and taxes included" on the campaign page.
5. **Add platform fees** on top of what you collect for shipping.
6. **Choose your pledge manager** and how VAT will be collected.
7. **Plan production around Chinese New Year** if fulfilment falls in the first quarter.
