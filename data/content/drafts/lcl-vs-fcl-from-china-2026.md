# LCL vs FCL from China: Where the Break-Even Really Sits in 2026

![Straddle carriers and stacked shipping containers in a rain-soaked container yard, where shared and full containers wait to be loaded](/images/blog/lcl-vs-fcl-from-china-2026.webp)

> **Quick answer:** **LCL** (less than container load) buys space in a shared container by the cubic metre. **FCL** (full container load) buys the whole box. LCL is cheaper for small shipments; FCL wins once a container costs less than the same space bought by the cubic metre plus LCL's fixed charges. Forwarders commonly put the crossover for a 20ft container at **around 12–18 CBM** from China to the US, but it is not fixed. It moves whenever rates move, and in 2026 they moved a lot. Work it out on same-day quotes: **break-even CBM = (FCL all-in − LCL fixed charges) ÷ LCL rate per CBM.** And before Chinese New Year, LCL closes first.

"Over 15 CBM, go full container" is one of those rules that is right often enough to survive and wrong often enough to cost money. It assumes a container costs roughly the same from one month to the next.

This year it did not. After the Strait of Hormuz closed to most shipping in March 2026, Far East to US West Coast spot rates rose 29% in about a month, according to Xeneta figures reported by FreightWaves on 2 April. A fixed rule cannot follow a market like that. The sum behind it can, and it takes two minutes.

## What you are actually buying

**LCL** puts your cargo in a container with other shippers' cargo. A consolidator books the whole container, fills it from many customers, and sells you the space. You pay by the cubic metre, or by the tonne if your cargo is heavy for its size, usually with a 1 CBM minimum.

**FCL** gives you the container. You pay the same whether it is full or half empty, so the cost per cubic metre falls with every carton you add.

| Container | Internal volume | Maximum payload |
|---|---|---|
| 20ft standard | about 33.2 m³ | about 28,100 kg |
| 40ft standard | about 67.7 m³ | about 28,700 kg |
| 40ft high cube | about 76.3 m³ | about 28,600 kg |

These are representative carrier figures and vary from box to box. You will load less than the internal volume, because cartons rarely fit the walls and ceiling exactly. Plan on what your cartons occupy, not on the brochure figure.

## The break-even sum

LCL has two kinds of cost. Some are **per cubic metre**: the ocean rate. Some are **fixed per shipment**: receiving at the origin consolidation warehouse, export documents, unloading and sorting at the destination warehouse, and the release paperwork. A full container has its own origin and destination charges, but they come in one all-in price.

So the volume at which FCL becomes cheaper is:

**Break-even CBM = (FCL all-in price − LCL fixed charges) ÷ LCL rate per CBM**

Below that volume, LCL costs less. Above it, take the container.

### A worked example

The numbers below are made up to show the method. They are not today's rates.

- LCL: **$100 per CBM**, plus **$400** of fixed charges per shipment
- A 20ft container to the same place: **$2,500** all-in

Break-even = ($2,500 − $400) ÷ $100 = **21 CBM**.

Now change one number. If the container price rises to $3,500 while the LCL quote stays put, the break-even jumps to **31 CBM**, more than you can realistically load into a 20ft. On those quotes, LCL beats a 20ft every time. If the container falls to $1,800 instead, the break-even drops to **14 CBM**.

Same cargo, same lane, and a break-even anywhere from 14 to 31 CBM depending on one quote. That is why the rule of thumb fails in a year like this, and why the only reliable answer comes from two quotes taken on the same day.

## Why the line moved in 2026

Container prices and LCL prices come from different places. A container rate comes from the carrier. An LCL rate comes from a consolidator who has already bought the box and is reselling the space, so the two do not always move at the same speed. When container rates jump faster than LCL quotes, the break-even rises. When they fall faster, it drops.

2026 gave plenty of movement. After the Hormuz closure in March, ship fuel in Singapore traded at roughly double its pre-crisis price, and Xeneta had Far East to US West Coast spot rates up 29% and Far East to North Europe up 31% since the end of February, as FreightWaves reported on 2 April. By 22 October Drewry had Shanghai to Los Angeles at **$8,007 per 40ft** and Shanghai to Rotterdam at **$3,632 per 40ft**, with its composite index at a 15-week high. In early October, forwarders were quoting shared-container cargo to the US West Coast at **$60–120 per CBM** base rate, before port and handling charges. Our [FBA freight rates page](fba-freight-from-china.html) carries the current market references by lane, refreshed monthly.

Carriers also kept cutting sailings to hold rates up. Drewry counted 10 blank sailings announced for a single week in early October. A blanked sailing hits LCL harder, because a consolidator cannot sail a half-full box without losing money on it.

## The costs LCL hides behind the per-CBM rate

**Fixed charges.** On a 3 CBM shipment, a few hundred dollars of fixed fees can cost more than the ocean freight itself. Always compare totals, not rates.

**Minimum charges.** Most consolidators charge for at least 1 CBM, and heavy cargo is charged by weight when it exceeds its volume.

**More handling.** LCL cargo is loaded into a shared box at origin and unloaded and sorted at destination. More touches mean more risk for fragile goods.

**Waiting for the box to fill.** A consolidator sails when the container is ready, so your cargo may wait at the warehouse.

**Shared examinations.** If customs examines a shared container, every shipment in it waits until the examination is done, including yours.

**An extra step for Amazon.** LCL cargo goes from the destination warehouse to Amazon by truck. A full container can often go straight to a fulfilment centre on a booked appointment. Our guide to [sea freight to Amazon FBA](sea-freight-to-amazon-fba-from-china-2026.html) walks through both routes.

## When to take a container below the break-even

The cheapest option is not always the right one. A full container is worth paying for when:

- **The goods are fragile or high value**, and fewer touches matter more than the saving
- **Timing matters**, because FCL skips the consolidation and unloading warehouses
- **The shipment is a single large Amazon delivery**, where a direct truck saves a cross-dock
- **The cargo includes batteries or other dangerous goods**, which many consolidators restrict. Our guide to [shipping batteries from China](shipping-batteries-electronics-from-china-2026.html) explains why

## Before Chinese New Year, LCL closes first

The CFS cut-off is the last date LCL cargo can arrive at the consolidation warehouse for a given sailing. It is normally about five days before the ship leaves. Before Chinese New Year it comes earlier, because warehouses close for the holiday and volumes peak, and forwarders warned of earlier cut-offs and longer lead times again for 2026.

How much earlier? In 2026, Scan Global Logistics listed Guangzhou's LCL cut-off as **4 February** and Shanghai's as 6 February, when New Year's Day was 17 February. That is 13 days ahead for Guangzhou. The same gap before **6 February 2027** would put South China LCL cut-offs around **24 January**. Treat that as a planning assumption until the 2027 notices appear, usually in January.

After the holiday, LCL services also suffer most from blank sailings while factories restart. Our [CNY 2027 deadlines guide](chinese-new-year-2027-shipping-deadlines.html) sets mid-January as the safe date to book and hand over all sea freight, and for LCL that date matters most.

## The quick decision table

| Your shipment | Usually the better choice |
|---|---|
| Under about 5 CBM | LCL, or air if it is urgent |
| About 5–12 CBM | LCL, unless the goods are fragile or time-critical |
| About 12–18 CBM | Run the sum on same-day quotes |
| Over about 18 CBM, or most of a 20ft | FCL |
| Several suppliers, each with a few CBM | Consolidate first, then decide |

That last row is where most sellers leave money behind. Five suppliers each shipping 4 CBM by LCL pay five sets of fixed charges for 20 CBM of cargo that would fit in one 20ft container.
