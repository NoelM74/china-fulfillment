# Shipping from China to Europe in 2026–27: Sea, Rail, Truck or Air?

![A China Railway Container Transport container on an X70 container flat wagon, part of a freight train waiting at a rural railway station](/images/blog/shipping-china-to-europe-sea-rail-truck-air-2026.webp)

> **Quick answer:** **Sea** is still the cheapest way to move bulk cargo from China to Europe, at **$3,632 per 40ft** Shanghai to Rotterdam on Drewry's 22 October 2026 index, but most sailings still go round Africa and take **30–45+ days**. **Rail** takes about **14–25 days** for around **$10,000 per 40ft** (Index1520, August 2026). **Truck** takes about **12–20 days** to Poland and Germany and is quoted per load. **Air** takes **4–7 days** at about **$4.80 per kg** from Hong Kong (TAC Index, September 2026). Choose by value per cubic metre and how long the stock can wait. Customs is the same whichever you pick.

For most of the last decade, the answer for bulk cargo to Europe was simply "sea, five or six weeks". It still is for heavy, low-value goods. For everything else the choice has got harder, because the sea route got longer, rail got busier and dearer, and both got less predictable.

This guide compares the four modes as they stand in late October 2026 and shows which suits which cargo. For our own rail and truck services, see the [China–Europe rail freight](china-europe-rail-freight.html) and [truck freight to Europe](china-europe-truck-freight.html) pages.

## The four modes side by side

| Mode | Market reference | Typical transit | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sea, full container** | $3,632 / 40ft Shanghai–Rotterdam; $4,152 / 40ft to Genoa (Drewry WCI, 22 Oct 2026) | 30–45+ days | Heavy or low-value goods, planned restocks | Cape routing, fuel and emissions surcharges |
| **Sea, shared container** | $60–150 / CBM base rate (forwarder quotes, 2026) | 30–45+ days, plus consolidation | Restocks under about 15 CBM | Fixed charges, earlier cut-offs |
| **Rail** | ~$10,000 / 40ft (Index1520, Aug 2026); groupage $160–210 / CBM (forwarders, early 2026) | 14–25 days | Valuable goods that cannot wait six weeks | One main border gateway, congestion |
| **Truck** | Quoted per load | 12–20 days to Poland and Germany | Urgent loads to Central and Eastern Europe | Border queues, limited capacity |
| **Air** | $4.80 / kg Hong Kong–Europe (TAC Index, Sep 2026) | 4–7 days airport to airport | Launches, stock-outs, small valuable lots | Charged on volume weight |

## What a cubic metre costs to move

Rates come in different units, which makes the modes hard to compare. Converting each to a cubic metre helps:

| Mode | Market reference per CBM |
|---|---|
| Sea, shared container | $60–150 base rate, before port and handling charges |
| Rail groupage | $160–210 |
| Air | about $800, for light cargo charged on volume weight |
| Truck | Quoted per load |

The air figure is arithmetic, not a quote. Airlines charge the higher of actual weight and volume weight, and one cubic metre counts as 167 kg, so a cubic metre of light goods at $4.80 per kg costs about $800 before surcharges. Divide each line by the number of units you fit into a cubic metre and you have the freight cost per unit, which is the number your margin actually feels.

## Sea: still the cheapest, now the slowest it has been in years

**Why it takes longer.** Red Sea transits remain well below normal, so most Asia–Europe container services still sail around the Cape of Good Hope rather than through Suez. That is the main reason transit runs 30 to 45 days or more. The Strait of Hormuz is not on the Asia–Europe route, but the crisis there since March 2026 pushed ship fuel prices up sharply, and fuel feeds straight into freight rates.

**Where rates are heading.** On 22 October, Drewry had Shanghai to Rotterdam at $3,632 per 40ft, up 3% on the week, and Shanghai to Genoa at $4,152, up 5%. It expected Asia–Europe rates to rise slightly the following week "amid tight capacity".

**The emissions surcharge.** From 2026, shipping lines must cover 100% of their reportable emissions under the EU Emissions Trading System, up from 70% in 2025. Voyages into or out of the EU count at half their emissions. Carriers pass the cost on as a surcharge: Maersk's for Far East to North Europe stood at **$168 per 40ft** in January 2026, up $54 in a quarter. It is small next to the freight rate, but it is on every invoice.

**When sea wins.** Heavy or bulky goods with modest value per cubic metre, and any restock planned far enough ahead to absorb six weeks or more. For Amazon sellers, our [sea freight to Amazon FBA guide](sea-freight-to-amazon-fba-from-china-2026.html) covers the steps, and our [LCL vs FCL guide](lcl-vs-fcl-from-china-2026.html) helps decide between a shared and a full container.

## Rail: half the time, about three times the price

**How it works.** Trains leave from Chinese hubs such as Xi'an, Chongqing, Chengdu, Wuhan and Yiwu. Most cross Kazakhstan, Russia and Belarus, change rail gauge at the borders, and enter the EU at **Małaszewicze** in Poland. A southern "Middle Corridor" via the Caspian Sea and Türkiye exists, but it is slower with more transfers. A policy brief published on 12 October 2026 called it "a useful option, not a replacement", noting that routes through Russia still carry about 90% of China–EU rail freight.

**Demand.** Rail grew sharply as shippers looked for alternatives to the long sea route. China's rail operator reported 5,460 China–Europe trains and 546,000 TEU in the first quarter of 2026, up 29% and 22% on a year earlier, as reported by Xinhua.

**Price.** Index1520 put the average China–Europe rail rate at about **$10,000 per 40ft** in August 2026, for carrier-owned containers, after a spring in which it described capacity on trains as extremely limited. That is close to three times the sea rate to Rotterdam, for about half the transit time.

**The risks.**

- **One gateway.** An estimated 85 to 90% of trains enter the EU at Małaszewicze. Poland closed its border with Belarus from 12 to 25 September 2025, which stopped most China–EU rail traffic for two weeks.
- **Congestion.** In its August 2026 update, Index1520 described the situation on routes through Poland and Germany as challenging, citing technical failures, terminal congestion and maintenance work.
- **Restricted goods.** Dual-use items and anything caught by EU sanctions cannot travel via Russia, and some brands and insurers avoid the route altogether. Batteries and other dangerous goods need approval in advance, and not every train accepts them.

**When rail wins.** Goods valuable enough that three weeks saved is worth the premium, and Amazon EU restocks that would miss a sale or a stock-out window by sea.

## Truck: the door-to-door option

Trucks run from China across Central Asia into Europe in about **12 to 20 days** to Poland and Germany, collecting at our dock and delivering to the destination warehouse without a change of mode. There is no public rate index for the lane, so every load is quoted. Trucking suits urgent full loads to Central and Eastern Europe, and inland destinations where a train or port is far away. It shares some of the same border exposure as rail, and capacity is far smaller.

## Air: when time beats everything

Air takes **4 to 7 days** airport to airport. The TAC Index put Hong Kong to Europe at **$4.80 per kg** in September 2026, well below the $7.02 per kg it recorded to North America. Remember that bulky goods are charged on volume weight. Air is the right call for launches, stock-outs and small lots of valuable goods. It is rarely the right call for a regular restock.

## Which mode for which cargo

| Your cargo | Usually the best mode |
|---|---|
| Heavy or low value, such as furniture, tools, homeware | Sea |
| Planned Amazon EU restock with six weeks or more of cover | Sea |
| Valuable goods that must land within about a month | Rail |
| Urgent full load to Poland, Germany or nearby | Truck |
| Launch stock, a stock-out, a small valuable lot | Air |
| Batteries or other dangerous goods | Sea or air with approval; rail only on approved trains |

## Customs is the same whichever you choose

EU duty, import VAT and the importer's obligations follow the goods, not the mode. A bulk shipment is cleared under the importer's EORI number, at the border or at the destination terminal, whether it arrived by ship, train, truck or plane.

Parcels sent directly to European consumers are a different regime. Since **1 July 2026** the EU charges a flat **€3 per item category** on low-value parcels, replacing the old €150 duty exemption, and our [EU de minimis guide](eu-de-minimis-2026-customs-duty-guide.html) explains how that works with IOSS. Changes to who acts as importer for distance sales are covered in our [EU customs reform guide](eu-customs-reform-importer-distance-sales-2026.html).

## Chinese New Year 2027 and Europe

Chinese New Year falls on **6 February 2027**, and Europe is the lane where it bites hardest, because the sea leg is so long. A sailing in mid-January, the last sensible booking window in our [CNY 2027 deadlines guide](chinese-new-year-2027-shipping-deadlines.html), reaches Northern Europe in late February at the earliest. If stock has to be in an Amazon EU warehouse before March, rail booked in January is often the only mode that fits. Book it early, because rail space before the holiday is limited.

## The bottom line

In 2026 the choice for Europe is no longer "sea, unless it is urgent". Sea is still about a third of the rail price per container, but it now takes up to six or seven weeks. Rail halves the time and adds risk at a single border. Truck and air fill the gaps. Convert every quote to cost per unit, decide how many weeks of stock you can afford to have on the water, and the right mode usually picks itself.
