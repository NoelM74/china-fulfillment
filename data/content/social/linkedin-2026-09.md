## POST 1 — Multi-channel fulfillment

**Suggested image:** the Shopify order dashboard shot, `images/site/shopify-order-dashboard-wms-china-fulfillment-integration.webp`

---

The worst time to find out your stock counts don't talk to each other is the hour a product finally takes off.

Twenty units sell on TikTok Shop before lunch. Shopify still shows the old number. So does Amazon. By the time anyone spots it you are cancelling orders from people who paid you in good faith, and the algorithm that just started favouring you is watching your cancellation rate climb.

Most sellers file this under "platform problem". It isn't. Shopify, TikTok Shop and Amazon each do their own job properly. It is an inventory problem. The same product is sitting in three separate counts and nothing is telling all three where the real number is.

We run it the other way round. One stock pool in our Shenzhen warehouse sits behind every channel you connect. A unit sells anywhere, the available quantity drops everywhere, at the same moment. No overnight sync job, no reconciliation spreadsheet, no separate safety buffer held per channel.

What that actually saves you:

You stop holding three lots of safety stock to guard against a problem a shared count removes.

A crowdfunding campaign and your everyday store draw from the same units, so a launch does not need its own reserved pile sitting idle.

Tracking numbers write back to each platform automatically, so your customer hears from your store rather than from a warehouse they have never heard of.

Shopify and WooCommerce are usually live in one to two business days. Amazon Seller Central takes two to three with SP-API. Anything without a direct connection runs on our API or a scheduled file upload.

One thing worth saying plainly, because the word gets stretched. This is multi-channel across DTC and marketplaces. If you need EDI into Walmart or Target replenishment, we do not do that, and we would rather tell you now than after you have signed.

If you are selling in more than two places and reconciling stock by hand, that is worth half an hour of anyone's time to fix.

## POST 2 — HS codes and tariff engineering

**Suggested image:** `images/blog/infographic-classification-traps.webp` (the five categories graphic) or the tariff engineering hero

---

Ford paid $365 million over a van seat.

The Transit Connect was built as a cargo van. Cargo vans into the US carry 25% duty. Passenger vehicles carry 2.5%. So Ford shipped them with a second row of seats fitted, declared them as passenger vehicles, and had a subcontractor take the seats out at the port before delivery.

Customs called it an artifice. The Court of International Trade sided with Ford. The Federal Circuit reversed it. Ford settled.

Now hold that next to Converse, which adds felt to the outsole of certain shoes. Felt changes how the shoe is classified under the footwear headings, and the duty rate follows. Completely lawful. The difference is not subtle once you see it: Converse sells the shoe it imported. Ford did not sell the van it imported.

That principle is older than most people assume. The Supreme Court settled it in 1882, when sugar refiners made their sugar darker because the tariff was keyed to colour. The Court's answer was blunt. Has not a manufacturer a right to make his goods as he pleases?

Here is why any of this matters if you import from China.

Your duty rate is not a fixed cost. It is the output of a classification decision. And in most businesses we talk to, nobody ever made that decision on purpose. A supplier put a code on an invoice years ago, a broker filed it, and it has been repeating on every shipment since.

That error runs both ways. If the code understates duty you owe the difference plus a penalty. If it overstates duty you have been quietly overpaying, and nobody at customs has any reason at all to tell you.

The fix costs nothing and takes an afternoon. Rank your products by duty actually paid over the last twelve months. Take the top ten. For each one, try to write down why that code is the right one. Most people cannot finish that table, and the gaps are the finding.

If a code genuinely moves your margin, you can simply ask. CBP's binding ruling process is free, most classification rulings come back within 30 calendar days, and the answer binds them at every US port.

Worth being straight about our own role. Classification is the importer of record's legal responsibility, not ours. What we do is capture the product data it rests on, materials, fibre content, weights, construction, and keep it consistent across every shipment. For a code you intend to rely on, use a licensed customs broker or a trade attorney. We work alongside them regularly and the combination works well.
