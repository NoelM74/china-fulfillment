The EU Customs Reform Makes You the Importer. Here Is When.

By Noel Murphy Published October 3, 2026

Quick answer: The new Union Customs Code, Regulation (EU) 2026/2108, entered into force on 21 September 2026. It creates a role called the importer for distance sales: on goods sold online to EU consumers from outside the EU, the importer is the seller or the platform, not the consumer. That covers a Shopify brand shipping direct from China as much as it covers a marketplace. The role applies from 1 July 2028, when the EU Customs Data Hub opens to e-commerce. The work to start now is your product data: descriptions, values and tariff codes accurate enough to put your name to.

For as long as most sellers have shipped into Europe, the person who legally imported a low-value parcel was the customer who ordered it.

That ends. The EU has rewritten its customs law, and the person it now holds responsible for a parcel crossing the border is the person who sold it. If you sell to European consumers from a warehouse in China, that is you.

What happened, and when

The European Parliament approved the reform on 16 September 2026. It was published in the Official Journal on Saturday 19 September 2026 as Regulation (EU) 2026/2108, and entered into force on Monday 21 September 2026.

It replaces Regulation (EU) 952/2013, the Union Customs Code that has governed EU customs since 2016. The Commission and the advisers following it describe it as the largest change to European customs since the customs union was created in 1968.

Entering into force is not the same as applying. The regulation switches on in stages, and the stages are what matter to a seller.

Date What happens
1 July 2026 Flat €3 customs duty on parcels under €150 replaces the old exemption. Already in effect
21 September 2026 New Union Customs Code enters into force
1 November 2026 New EU handling fee must be operational, at the latest
2027 EU Customs Authority established, headquartered in Lille
21 September 2027 General application of the new Code
1 July 2028 Importer for distance sales applies: Customs Data Hub opens to e-commerce, the €3 interim duty is replaced
1 March 2034 Customs Data Hub mandatory for all importers

The role that changes everything: importer for distance sales

The reform introduces a new legal concept. For goods sold at a distance to EU consumers and shipped from outside the EU, the importer is the importer for distance sales, defined as either:

  • the person supplying the goods in the distance sale, or
  • the person facilitating the sale, meaning an online marketplace or platform.

Whoever holds the role takes responsibility for the customs formalities and for showing that the goods comply with customs, product safety and other import requirements.

Sit with what that replaces. Under the old arrangement the customer was the importer of their own parcel, even though they had never filled in a customs form in their life. Customs liability landed on the one party with no knowledge of what was in the box. The reform moves it to the party that does know: the one who sold it.

"That is for Temu and Shein, not me"

This is the misreading worth heading off, because the coverage encourages it.

The reform is widely reported as a crackdown on marketplaces, and platforms are certainly its headline target. But the definition does not stop at platforms. The person supplying the goods is named in the same breath.

If you run your own Shopify or WooCommerce store, sell to customers in Germany, France or Italy, and ship from a warehouse in China, there is no marketplace in the chain. You are the person supplying the goods. You are inside the definition.

If you sell through a marketplace, the platform may take the role instead. How that allocation works in practice, and what it means for the data the platform will demand from you, is detail still being worked through. Expect platforms to push the data burden back to sellers either way, because they will not accept liability for descriptions they did not write.

When it applies, and why that is not long

The importer for distance sales role applies from 1 July 2028. That is the day the EU Customs Data Hub opens to e-commerce, and the Commission's timeline brings the e-commerce changes in with it.

Do not confuse it with 21 September 2027, which is the general application date for the reform as a whole. That date matters to customs in general; for a seller shipping parcels to consumers, July 2028 is the one that changes who the importer is.

Twenty-one months sounds like plenty. It is not, if your catalogue has never been classified properly or your product descriptions were written for a marketplace listing rather than a customs declaration. That work takes months to do well, and the sellers who start in 2028 will be doing it under pressure.

What actually has to be ready

The role is new. The work behind it is not. It is the same work good sellers already do, with your name now attached to the result.

Accurate product descriptions. Not "gift" or "accessories". What the item is, what it is made of, what it is for. A vague description was a nuisance under the old rules. Under the new ones it is your declaration.

Correct tariff classification. Every SKU needs the right HS code, applied consistently. If you have never had your catalogue classified properly, this is the single most valuable thing to fix well before July 2028. Our HS code classification service exists for exactly this, and the common classification mistakes are a fast way to find out where you stand.

True values. Declared values that match what the customer actually paid. Undervaluation was always a risk. As the importer it is your risk directly.

Product compliance evidence. The importer has to show the goods comply with product safety rules, not just customs rules. If you sell anything that needs a CE mark, test reports or a responsible person in the EU, that paperwork moves from nice to have to required. Our guide to product compliance from China covers what applies to which categories.

A clean IOSS setup. If you already collect VAT at checkout through IOSS, keep it current. Sellers already running IOSS properly are the ones best placed for the move to the Data Hub.

What it means for the €3 duty

Since 1 July 2026, parcels under €150 have paid a flat €3 customs duty in place of the old exemption. We covered the mechanics and the checkout fixes in our EU de minimis guide.

The Commission describes that €3 as temporary. It is due to be replaced by normal duty treatment once the Data Hub's e-commerce phase is running from July 2028. So the cheap, flat rate is a bridge, not a destination. If your EU margins currently depend on it, model what happens when real tariff rates apply to your real HS codes.

Where we sit in this, and where we do not

We are a fulfilment provider in Shenzhen. We store, pack and ship, and we prepare the shipment documentation that travels with your goods.

We are not your importer for distance sales, we are not a customs representative, and handling a parcel does not make us the importer of it. Under this reform the role sits with the seller or the platform. Nobody can take it off you by shipping your goods.

What we can do is make sure the data that leaves our warehouse is accurate and consistent: the descriptions, the values, the classifications, matched SKU by SKU. When the importer's name on the declaration is yours, that consistency is what protects you. It is the core of our tariff management service, and if you are selling into Germany specifically, our China to Germany fulfilment guide covers the market detail.

For the legal side, meaning representation, liability and how the role applies to your specific setup, use a licensed customs representative or a customs adviser in the EU. That is their work, not ours.

The bottom line

Europe has decided that the person who sells the goods should answer for them at the border. For a brand shipping from China direct to European customers, that means you, on a date that is close enough to plan for now.

The good news is that nothing on the to-do list is new. Accurate descriptions, correct codes, true values, compliance paperwork you can actually produce. The difference is that from now on it is your declaration, not your customer's.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the new Union Customs Code?

It is Regulation (EU) 2026/2108, which replaces Regulation (EU) 952/2013 as the legal basis for customs across the EU. It was published in the Official Journal on 19 September 2026 and entered into force on 21 September 2026. Its provisions apply in phases, with general application from 21 September 2027 and the e-commerce changes tied to the EU Customs Data Hub from 1 July 2028. It also creates a new EU Customs Authority, based in Lille.

What is an importer for distance sales?

It is a new role created by the reform. For goods sold online to EU consumers and shipped from outside the EU, the importer is either the seller supplying the goods or the platform facilitating the sale. That person takes responsibility for the customs formalities and for showing the goods comply with customs, product safety and other import rules. The consumer stops being the importer.

When does the importer for distance sales rule apply?

From 1 July 2028, the date the EU Customs Data Hub opens to e-commerce and the European Commission's timeline brings in the e-commerce changes. That is a separate milestone from the Code's general application date of 21 September 2027, which covers the wider reform. Confirm the position for your own business with a customs adviser.

Does this apply to Shopify sellers shipping direct from China, or only to marketplaces?

Both. The definition covers the person supplying goods in distance sales as well as the platform facilitating them. Marketplaces are the headline case, but a brand selling through its own Shopify or WooCommerce store and shipping from a warehouse in China to EU consumers is the seller supplying the goods, which puts it inside the definition.

What happens to the 3 euro customs duty on low-value parcels?

Since 1 July 2026 a flat 3 euro customs duty has applied to parcels valued under 150 euros, replacing the old duty exemption. The European Commission describes it as temporary. It is due to be replaced by normal duty treatment once the Customs Data Hub's e-commerce phase is running from 1 July 2028.

Is my fulfilment provider the importer for distance sales?

No. The role sits with the seller supplying the goods or the platform facilitating the sale. A fulfilment provider stores, packs and ships goods and can prepare accurate shipment data, but it does not become the importer because it handled the parcel. Customs representation and compliance advice should come from a licensed customs representative or adviser.
